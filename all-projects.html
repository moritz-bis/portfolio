2024
Obertshausen, Germany
harolds-bags.com

The leather tie was designed for Harold's Lederwaren GmbH, as their first product made purely from production waste. It is a sustainable and aesthetic reinterpretation of the classic zip tie.

origin ->
When manufacturing bags from natural materials like leather, offcuts and production waste are inevitable. One typical example is the grid-like pattern left behind after die-cutting. These narrow stripes are left standing to ensure clean and precise edges.

prototyping ->
The idea was simple: turn these stripes into leather zip ties. However, to do this every detail had to be carefully thought out. The closure mechanism was particularly crucial: it needed to be functional, visually appealing, and cost-effective. Through this testing one solution quickly stood out – two simple slits in the leather create an intuitive yet sturdy lock.

final product ->
Finally I fine tuned this by working out the right measurements and enforcing the tip with natural wax. The final zip tie can be used in a multitude of ways and is already readily in use at Harold's because of its functionality and aesthetic.

humus

bio-waste isn't waste

2024
HfG Offenbach, Germany

This project reimagines organic waste recycling as an aesthetic and decentralised process. It literally brings the issue into the public eye using a system of composters in public spaces. It emphasises that organic waste should not be seen as a problem, but rather as a valuable natural resource.

research ->
Bio-waste is a valuable resource e.g. as organic fertiliser. But on its way from food scraps to fertiliser, it can create tremendous greenhouse gas emissions. Today’s recycling systems require extensive transportation, which makes the system unprofitable and enviromentally problematic. (see EA report 04/2020)

reimagining trash cans ->
The waste container is the symbol of Bio-waste, so changing the image of Bio-waste means changing the image of the waste container. The module has to emphasises the reuse of the compost and break the visual garbage bin connection.

a new waste bin ->
The reimagined waste bin strongly resembles a planting which shifts the association away from waste towards reuse. Form and material have been choosen accordingly and plants can be planted on top of the module, creating an local circularity. Since organic waste doesn’t smell bad once it is composted, the system could even be placed in new contexts such as public spaces.

a new system ->
A new Waste container, that composts at site also allowed a complete redesign of the system of bio-waste managment. Decentralising and only transporting the resulting compost substantially reduces the enviromental impact while digital technology makes managing it easy.

technicalities ->
The module utilises the so called continuous flow-through vermi-composting principal, a proven system for industrial composters. The composter sits beneath the greenery. Fresh bio- waste is added to the top, while the finished humus can be harvested through a grid at the bottom. This makes the system easy to use and efficient. Sensors monitor the process and, connected trough a wireless network, create a decentralised composting system.

V-bin

making zero waste stores affordable

2021
HfG Offenbach, Germany

While zero waste stores and unpackaged selling are bound to be the future of sustainable shopping, they are still unaffordable for many. V-bin aims to solve that problem by focusing on re-designing the dispensing system, making it less work-intensive.

the problem ->
Buying unpackaged goods means buying less, since you are not buying the packaging. The reality however is, that zero waste shops are substantially more expensive. The target of this project was to adress this contradiction and making zero waste an affordable alternative.

a new system ->
V-bin drastically simplifies the workload of zero waste and is planned as an intervention that makes transitioning easy. When implementing V-bin dispensers, shops can first simply switch to the wholesale packages of their favourite brands, already reducing plastic waste by about 60%. Once several stores have the dispensers in place, manufactures can start using reusable textile bags, relying on an already existing customer base.

the valve ->
In terms of the design, the key piece of each module is a textile iris valve, similar to those widely used in agriculture. The redesigned handle allows the user to easily dispense food into a container or bag, with minimal direct contact.

construction ->
The iris valve clamps shut or releases the 5-20kg bags, which are held in place by a simple funnel. The user places their bag over the wire handle, eliminating direct contact, and pulls the notch to open the valve. Thanks to this simple construction all already existing large bag formats are compatible with the system.

|X|X|X|

a door that isolates while feeling open

2020
HfG Offenbach, Germany

A literal open door is a very powerful image and social sign. Yet, in a shared living situation you often have to close your room door because of simple things like noise or smells. |X|X|X| addresses this discrepancy by offering an intermediated state of being physically closed but visually open.

assistance through co-living ->
Despite technology advancing faster than ever, humans are still the undefeated champion of helping each other. The central idea of |X|X|X| is therefore to foster co-living. A key player for this, is the room door, seperating private and shared but also communitcating solitude or openness.

functional hinge ->
The central characteristic of |X|X|X| is its textile hinge. Stiffened fabric folded in an origami pattern not only holds the glass panes in place but also lends it its distinctive properties. Because of its minimalistic construction the door could be easily retrofitted into any home.

one door - three stages ->
Unlike conventional doors, |X|X|X| has three states: open, closed and an intermediate stage of physically closed but visually open. When pulled shut, the sliding door isolates the room with its glass panes, while still letting light permeate and allowing glimpses from the outside world. If the user wishes for more privacy, the door can be pulled shut even further making it fully opaque.

paravent

adaptive guidance in public buildings

2019
HfG Offenbach, Germany
realised together with fellow student Luke Handon

Paravent is designed for public spaces like big train stations, where adaptive crowd guiding is as important as the right spatial atmosphere. The human sized panels are able to rotate, allowing the space to be adapted to the changing demands through out the day.

research and surveys ->
This project was initiated by the DB, Germanys national railway company. They were looking for new ideas to solve the problems of Frankfurt central station. We started by conducting on-site surveys, talking to passengers throughout the day and reading about the topic.

public spaces are dynamic ->
The research revealed, that a train station is a very dynamic place where needs are constantly changing. Even within a few hundred meters or within a couple of hours, crowds have significantly different needs. Rush hour for example demands a highly efficient organisation, while during the day most people need a pleasant space to wait. This dynamic also translates to other public spaces.

adaptive funcionality ->
To allow for efficient crowd guiding as well as pleasant calm spots, we utilised the so-called niche-effect. It states, that humans appreceiate a pleasent corner or niche when waiting or relaxing. This meant we could use the same modular wall element to guide crowds and to create calm areas.

the module ->
The modular wall element, consists of an opaque glass pane, pivoted in its frame, so that it can turn around its vertical axis. Several modules are interlocked side by side, creating a dynamic wall, which can then be controlled remotely. This allows intuitive guiding, niche construction and adaptive routes e.g. in case of emergency - all with one module.

microplaza

guiding the flow of people and water

2022
HfG Offenbach, Germany
realised together with fellow students Domenico Bösterling and Patrick Witkowski

Cities without traffic can free up a lot of space for people and greenery. But this space still needs to be functional. By using a topography based upon a generative layout “microplaza” creates a subtle guidance for people as well as water.

superblocks in Offenbach ->
The city of Offenbach am Main showed interest in applying the superblock-concept to the Goetheplatz in Offenbach’s Nordend. Microplaza creates a possible framework for that. Inspired by the superblocks of Barcelona, it iterates on the idea and focuses on street-level layout.

parametric generation ->
Using the house entrances as magnetic anchor points a parametric line network is generated. The island layout is created based upon these lines and a fixed street width of 3m, allowing emergency traffic like firetrucks. Finally the islands are assigned different functions from greenery, social space to utility.

principals ->
By introducing a topography three levels are created: elevated islands as social space, a sidewalk at ground level and lowered pools of greenery and utilities. The elevation clearly marks the calm areas but also guides the flow of people around the “hills”. Lowering the utility sections, such as bike parking, further supports the feeling of an overview over the area. At the same time water will naturally flow towards the lowered parts where the open ground and plants can naturally absorb it.

model (1:50) and render ->
A model made from lasercut cardboard as well as a render viualsizes the concept and was used to present it to the public.
